Ph.D. student · Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute

A F M Saif

Machine learning researcher working on LLM post-training, reinforcement learning, and agentic AI.

I study what happens to large models after pre-training: how to improve them with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, how to adapt them cheaply to new languages and tasks, and how to combine specialized models without losing what each one learned.

My current work looks at agents that learn from their own experience. Not every retrieved memory helps, and some make an agent worse, so I work on deciding which experiences to keep and how to keep shared memory safe. Earlier, I used bilevel and multi-objective optimization to balance competing objectives in multilingual speech and foundation models.

I enjoy both the math and the training loop. At IBM Research in summer 2026, I built SFT and GRPO pipelines for 8–9B-parameter models from scratch in PyTorch.

Research

Four connected threads, from how models are trained to how they are adapted and evaluated.

01Post-training and reinforcement learning for LLMs

Improving a model after pre-training requires control of the whole loop: data, rollouts, rewards, memory limits, and evaluation. I write that loop myself rather than relying on off-the-shelf trainers, which makes failures easier to diagnose.

At IBM Research I reproduced post-training pipelines for Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B, implemented SFT and GRPO for mathematical reasoning (rollout generation, group-relative advantages, and KL regularization to a reference policy), and trained with FSDP and DeepSpeed on multi-GPU nodes. I also evaluated model-merging methods, including Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, Fisher merging, RegMean, and Localize-and-Stitch, and sparse parameter adaptation for combining independently post-trained models with less cross-task interference.

Context Industry research, IBM Research, summer 2026

02Agentic AI: experience selection and memory safety

Agents can improve without weight updates by reusing experience from earlier tasks. But some retrieved experiences help, some hurt, and bad memories can spread when memory is shared.

I build training-free GRPO pipelines that optimize an external experience library with verifier-based rewards, evaluated on HumanEval, AIME, and HotpotQA with Qwen2.5 and DeepSeek-R1-Distill models. To decide which experiences to keep, I use credit-assignment methods such as Shapley values, cardinality-restricted Shapley values, greedy selection, retrieval, and LLM-as-a-judge. I also study harmful-memory propagation and the robustness of agents that read from external or shared memory.

Related Harmful memories in agentic LLMs (preprint) Research code: Shapley-guided experience refinement

03Efficient adaptation of foundation models

Fully fine-tuning a multi-billion-parameter model for every language or task is expensive and causes interference. I work on adaptation methods that train a small number of parameters and route inputs to the right ones.

BELLA connects a speech encoder to a Qwen2.5-7B decoder through a Q-Former bridge and adds mixture-of-experts LoRA with task-specific and shared experts. It trains roughly 72M parameters. Routing and adaptation are learned jointly as a bilevel problem with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2 on 8×A100 GPUs, and decoding combines candidate generation with LLM-based rescoring.

Related BELLA, Interspeech 2026 Heterogeneous SSL pre-training, ICASSP 2026

04Bilevel and multi-objective optimization for learning

Most training involves more than one objective. Self-supervised and supervised losses pull in different directions, languages and tasks compete for capacity, users prefer particular trade-offs, and hyperparameters sit inside the training process itself. I treat these as structured optimization problems rather than loss weights to hand-tune.

With collaborators, I replaced the usual pre-train-then-fine-tune pipeline with joint bilevel training for speech recognition, used conflict-aware multi-objective optimization to train one model for recognition and translation across languages, and helped develop methods for preference-guided multi-objective learning and for gradient-based hyperparameter tuning in diffusion models.

Related Objective Soups, NeurIPS 2025 Generative bilevel optimization, ICML 2025 FERERO, NeurIPS 2024 M²ASR, Interspeech 2024 BL-JUST, ICASSP 2024
